One patient, one bad week

What HeartSafe does, told through the 90 days of one patient called Mary.

The problem HeartSafe solves

People with heart failure go home from hospital with a scale, a blood-pressure cuff and an app. Every day their readings go to a remote monitoring platform, which raises an alert when something looks wrong.

The platform is good at raising alerts. It is not built to ask "is this reading even real?", to make sure someone acts on the alert, or to notice that patients in remote communities are served worse.

🔍

Check

Is the reading, the ECG or the MRI trustworthy? A wrong scale should not send a nurse running.

📝

Explain

Every escalation comes with a short review: why it was flagged, what can and cannot be trusted.

📦

Track

Each case has deadlines, reminders and a log, and follows the instruction all the way to the patient.

⚖️

Watch fairness

Compares urban, rural and remote patients and proposes fixes when one group falls behind.

💡
A layer, not a replacement

HeartSafe sits on top of an existing platform and reads its feed. This is a common product strategy: instead of rebuilding what works, you add the missing piece around it. When you brief an AI tool, saying "this is an adapter layer over an existing system" changes everything it suggests.

Mary's 90 days

Mary is 68, lives in a remote community, uses her own scale and has a single-lead ECG device. She is the scripted demo patient inside a simulated group of 2,001 patients.

20
Weight jumps +2.4 kg overnight

No symptoms, steady heart rate. HeartSafe suspects a new scale and asks for a repeat reading instead of escalating.

21
The jump is still there

A nurse is asked to call Mary and confirm the new scale. Weight-only alerts wait for that answer.

22
Nurse confirms: new scale

Readings are re-baselined. The next five days of platform alerts are filtered out as data problems.

46
Three days with no readings

An outreach task goes to the team: check the device, the connection, or whether Mary needs help.

50
A real episode starts

Weight up, breathless, swollen ankles. HeartSafe checks the ECG and opens a tracked clinical case with a review.

54
Her MRI comes back from a regional scanner

The scanner differs from the ones the model was trained on and the model is uncertain, so the result goes to expert review.

66
The loop closes

After her caregiver is contacted and the instruction is followed, her readings improve and the case is closed.

The heartbeat of the code

This is the main loop of the agent, in agent/heartsafe/agent.py. It walks through every patient, day by day, exactly like the journey above.

            for row in g.sort_values("day").itertuples():                now = row.day * 24 + 9.0                self._check_verification(p, st, row, now)                if not row.submitted:                    self._check_loops(p, st, row, now)                    st.missed += 1                    if st.missed == r["missing_outreach_days"]:                        review = build_review(p, row.day, [f"no readings for {st.missed} days"], st.hist, None,                                              "low", ["missed_readings"], [], kind="outreach", ack_h="{ack}")                        cid, _ = self._create_case(p, st, now, "outreach", "low", review)                        self._record(row, "outreach", severity="low", case_id=cid, flags="missed_readings")                    continue                st.missed = 0                today = self._today(row, st.offset)                self._check_loops(p, st, row, now, today)                if row.alert_level > 0:                    self._handle_alert(p, st, row, today, now)
PLAIN ENGLISH

Go through this patient's days in order.

Turn the day number into a clock time: 9 am on that day, counted in hours.

First, check whether a nurse has answered an open "is this a new scale?" question.

If no reading arrived today…

…still check any patient follow-ups that are due…

…and add one to the count of missed days in a row.

After three missed days (the number comes from the config file)…

…write a short review saying how many days are missing…

…

…open a low-priority "outreach" case…

…and record that decision.

Skip to the next day.

A reading arrived, so reset the missed-day count.

Build today's reading, correcting the weight if a new scale was confirmed.

Check follow-ups that are due now that a reading exists.

Did the platform raise an alert today?

Then hand it to the alert handler, which decides what to do (Module 3).

🕒
A simulated clock

The code never reads the real time. It uses "hours since monitoring began" so a 90-day study runs in about a minute, and the same seed always gives the same story. Engineers call this a deterministic simulation.
